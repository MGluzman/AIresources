About the program

Build practical AI literacy into your course

The AI Literacy Faculty Cohort provides faculty with training, guidance, and hands-on support as they develop AI literacy activities and assessments for their courses.

Faculty will design a course component that strengthens students’ career readiness by developing AI proficiency through responsible AI use, information literacy, critical thinking, and effective prompting. These components will emphasize meaningful AI integration that supports student learning rather than cognitive surrender.

Over four weeks, faculty will work as a cohort, with opportunities for collaboration, feedback, and hands-on support throughout the development process. Participants will implement their AI literacy component in a Spring 2027 course and, at the end of the semester, submit their updated syllabus, anonymous student survey results, and a brief reflection on their experience.

Participants will include a description of their AI literacy course component in their Spring 2027 syllabus and share the syllabus as an open educational resource (OER) under a Creative Commons BY-NC-SA license. They will also be encouraged to submit their full activity or assessment for inclusion in an OER publication developed by Library & Academic IT.

The pilot cohort is limited to 10 faculty participants to allow for more personalized support and facilitate productive hands-on work and collaboration.

Program note: The program scope and support available to participants may expand depending on the outcome of our AI Innovation Grant proposal.

Learn More: Frequently Asked Questions

Who can apply?

The pilot is open to full-time and part-time Brooklyn College faculty who plan to teach during the Spring 2027 semester. The cohort is limited to 10 faculty participants.

Who is this program best suited for?

The cohort is designed for faculty who want to explore purposeful ways to incorporate AI literacy into student learning.

Participants should have some prior experience using generative AI and writing prompts. The program will build on those foundational skills rather than introduce generative AI from the beginning.

What is the application deadline?

Applications are due November 15, 2026.

When will the program start?

The exact meeting schedule will be confirmed once the cohort is selected. The program will begin the week of November 30, 2026. Participants will work toward completing a finished Spring 2027 syllabus by January 17, 2027, including a description of their AI literacy course component.

What is the program duration?

The active cohort program will run for four weeks (late November 2026 through mid-January 2027, with breaks). Participants will then implement their AI literacy component in a live course during the Spring 2027 semester and complete the student survey and reflection phase at the end of the semester.

What is the time commitment?

During the active phase of the program, participants should expect to spend a minimum of three hours per week on cohort activities. This time will include learning, participating in workshops or development labs, asynchronous collaboration, and designing and refining their course component.

During Spring 2027, participants will implement the completed component in their course, administer an anonymous student survey, and submit the results with a brief reflection at the end of the semester.

How is the program structured?

This is a hybrid, working cohort rather than a traditional workshop series. Faculty will learn and experiment together while developing learning activities and assignments for their students.

The four-week experience will combine hands-on learning, workshops or development labs, collaborative work, feedback, asynchronous activities, and dedicated course-design time.

What will faculty be expected to design?

Each participant will develop one AI literacy course component, either a formative learning activity or a graded assessment, for implementation in a Spring 2027 course.

The component should strengthen students’ career readiness by building AI proficiency through responsible AI use, information literacy, critical thinking, and effective prompting. It should emphasize meaningful AI integration that supports student learning rather than cognitive surrender.

What will faculty learn?

Faculty will strengthen their own AI fluency while exploring practical approaches to designing AI-integrated learning activities and assessments for their students.

The program will explore areas such as AI-enhanced course design and workflows, student AI literacy, human-centered AI pedagogy, responsible AI use, digital accessibility, and emerging approaches to teaching and learning with AI. Participants will encounter strategies, examples, and pedagogical practices that can inform their own course design.

The program will emphasize evidence-informed pedagogy while also examining emerging practices in AI-supported teaching and learning.

What technologies and applications will we use?

Participants will use several familiar CUNY-supported technologies as part of the cohort.

Core tools will include:

  • Microsoft 365 Copilot or Copilot Chat
  • Microsoft Word
  • OneDrive
  • Brightspace
  • LinkedIn Learning
  • Zoom

Other tools may be introduced or used depending on the activity or individual project, including:

  • Microsoft PowerPoint
  • Microsoft Sway
  • Microsoft Loop
  • additional approved tools as appropriate

The focus will be on using these technologies to support AI literacy, course design, collaboration, and content development, rather than learning the applications for their own sake.

What kind of support will be available?

Faculty will receive guidance, hands-on support, opportunities for peer collaboration, and feedback as they develop their course components.

The cohort is intentionally limited to 10 participants to facilitate more personalized support, productive hands-on work, and collaboration.

The program scope and support available to participants may expand depending on the outcome of our AI Innovation Grant proposal.

What will participants be expected to submit or share?

Participants will:

  • develop and implement their AI literacy component in a Spring 2027 course;
  • share their Spring 2027 syllabus with a description of their AI literacy component as an OER under a Creative Commons BY-NC-SA license; and
  • administer an anonymous student survey and submit the results with a brief reflection at the end of the semester.

Faculty are also encouraged to consider submitting their complete activity or assessment for inclusion in an OER publication developed by Brooklyn College Library & Academic IT.
